# Model identities and recipes

Extracted verbatim from the authoritative supplement S2; weights are obtained from each cited author release, never bundled here. Historical checkpoint paths remain archive requirements.

## S2 Model implementations and training recipes

### Controlled CoPRA and additive comparator

Protein families were single-linkage components of sequence relationships meeting at least 40% identity and 70% coverage. Global alignment used BLOSUM62, with gap-opening and extension scores of −11 and −1. Identity counted identical residues among aligned non-gap columns; coverage divided aligned non-gap columns by the longer sequence length. Every family remained in one of five outer folds.

The principal experiment, denoted CoPRA (controlled), comprised five folds, three seeds and two methods, giving 30 fits. Each fit used absolute labels from the 771-unit training manifest outside the held-out fold, including units outside the local subset. Both methods optimized MSE with Adam at learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁵, batch size two and 30,000 steps, using the final checkpoint. Public ESM2-t33-650M (Lin et al. 2023) and RiNALMo-giga (Penić et al. 2025) encoders were frozen in evaluation mode. Co-Former and affinity-readout weights were initialized afresh; PRI30k complex-pretrained task weights were not loaded. CoPRA denotes this controlled configuration in the evaluation figures and tables. Training used seeds 20260916, 20260917 and 20260918.

CoPRA (Han et al. 2025) used its structure-integrating framework with structure-selected input regions and patch size 256. Its additive comparator shared the encoders and input selection. Masked representations were pooled separately over the protein and RNA sides, passed through separate 128-unit ReLU readouts, and added with a learned bias:

\[
\widehat y_{\mathrm{add}}=b+f_P(\overline h_P)+f_R(\overline h_R).
\]

This comparator estimates affinity from separate protein and RNA terms. The two complete recipes share data, folds, encoder states and optimization settings; their task modules and capacities differ.

Adam used betas (0.9, 0.999), zero weight decay and gradient clipping at 100. There was no inner-fold learning-rate selection, early stopping or learning-rate scheduler. Task optimization used FP32; frozen encoder forward passes retained CUDA autocast where implemented.

### DeePNAP

DeePNAP (Pandey et al. 2024) used the architecture of the authors' released deployment code with fresh random initialization and was retrained on absolute affinity labels from sequence inputs alone. Each fit held out the outer family fold and minimised absolute MSE plus the network's deployed L1 terms, weighting each confirmed absolute unit once, with batch size 64, Adam at learning rate 10⁻³, 200 epochs (5,200 steps) and no learning-rate schedule or early stopping; the final step was retained. Five family folds and the three fixed seeds gave 15 out-of-fold fits, and outer-fold labels were never used for training or checkpoint selection. Its sequence-additive comparator used frozen, masked-mean-pooled ESM2-t33-650M (layer 33) and RiNALMo-giga representations, with independent 128-unit ReLU readouts for the protein and RNA terms plus a learned bias, trained for 30,000 steps at learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁵, batch size two, Adam betas (0.9, 0.999) and zero weight decay, again retaining the final step. The secondary analysis intersected actual OOF coverage for these methods, CoPRA and the CoPRA additive comparator across all three seeds, then reassessed B-shape eligibility.

### OmniBioTE

The model-expansion analysis used two distinct checkpoints from the OmniBioTE release (Chen et al. 2026). The official application used `omnibinder-small.pt` (MD5 `728c3b5482fee12061918d01f9a75a1f`); the representation adaptation used `omnibiota-multi-E-mixed-small.pt` (MD5 `9c556af61281bebeb40469eae10260bd`). Their affinity supervision histories differ. The two configurations are not counted as independent prediction methods.

Inputs used the official tokenizer, RNA U→T conversion and the recorded multistrand convention. Inputs exceeding 1,024 combined tokens were rejected without truncation. The official application used FP32 forward computation from bf16-stored parameters, evaluation mode, SDPA math and disabled TF32. Repeated application batches gave identical outputs; the first saved batch supplies the application results. Its output head includes the published inverse standardization. Raw ΔG in kcal/mol was mapped to `pKd_ref = −ΔG/[R × 298 × ln(10)]`, where the denominator is approximately 1.36356 kcal/mol. The supervised training membership remains unresolved, and 298 K is a reference coordinate rather than a reconstruction of the temperature of every measurement.

The adaptation froze the base encoder in evaluation mode at its stored bf16 parameter precision, extracted the first-token representation, and converted this representation to FP32 for a new pure linear pKd head. Four direct-versus-cache checks covered short, median-length, long and double-stranded inputs and returned identical representations and head predictions. Label-free caching did not update the encoder or fit statistics across observations. Each measurement retained a separate identity, including when inputs shared a cache entry.

Five outer family folds and seeds 20260916, 20260917 and 20260918 defined 15 fits. Each fit trained on the other folds' encodable measurement units with equal-unit MSE, batch size eight including the final incomplete batch, and shuffled epochs. The head used normal(0,0.02) weights, zero bias and no μP scaling. AdamW used learning rate 0.01, weight decay 0.01, betas (0.9,0.999) and epsilon 1e−8. The fixed 64-epoch final state supplied predictions, without selection using the outer test labels. Both sides of a pair used the same fold-specific head and seed. Errors were computed by seed before equal averaging. Endpoint identity and numerical reproduction checks accompany the saved outputs.

### SVSBI GBNL and ProRB

SVSBI (Shen et al. 2023) was reconstructed under an author-approved plan, with unresolved implementation details specified by the present study. ESM-1b (`esm1b_t33_650M_UR50S`) supplied the mean of layer-33 residue representations, excluding special tokens and padding (1,280 features). RNA was uppercased, converted from U to T, and tokenized into overlapping 3-mers at stride one for the original DNABERT-3 (`zhihan1996/DNA_bert_3`). Its final-layer pooler used [CLS] → linear → tanh (768 features). Both encoders were frozen, evaluated without gradients in FP32, and concatenated into 2,048 features. Pooling and truncation are fixed reconstruction choices. Protein input retained the first 1,022 residues; RNA retained the first 510 3-mers, corresponding to 512 nucleotides. Multi-chain inputs were recorded as unsupported, and eight units with RNA too short for a 3-mer were excluded from encoding.

The regressor used 10,000 trees, learning rate 0.01, maximum depth 7, minimum split size 3, subsample 0.7, `max_features="sqrt"`, squared-error loss, Friedman-MSE split criterion and no early stopping. These settings were asserted before every fit. Fifteen fits used the existing five family folds and seeds 20260916, 20260917 and 20260918; training-fold row counts were 321, 332, 320, 354 and 265. The frozen 398-unit training manifest retained seven censored boundary labels; the study author confirmed that these had no material effect on the final SVSBI predictions. The reconstruction used Python 3.11.5, CPU PyTorch 2.13.0, fair-esm 2.0.0, transformers 5.17.0 and scikit-learn 1.8.0. The version differences from the original paper and checkpoint-loading adjustments are recorded in the local environment and verification files. All 199 DNABERT `bert.*` parameter keys, including its pooler, matched.

ProRB (Xue et al. 2026) used the v2.2 source tree and the author's supplied affinity checkpoint and inference files. Inference used α = 0.8, FP32 and batch size four; labels were not consumed. Its actual content windows were 1,022 protein residues and 510 RNA nucleotides, with multi-chain inputs recorded as unsupported. An isolated environment used GPU PyTorch 2.9.1+cu128, multimolecule 0.0.6, esm 3.2.1.post1, rna-fm 0.2.2 and transformers 4.48.1. The RNA-FM initialization required documented key-name and vocabulary compatibility adaptation from 28 to 26 tokens. Canonical A/C/G/U token IDs were preserved, and the complete ProRB checkpoint loaded strictly. Compatibility checks covered these canonical inputs, author-example execution and batch-size consistency. Author reference predictions were unavailable. Scores describe the supplied checkpoint application. The study author confirmed that no training overlap or leakage was identified for this evaluation.

GBNL (Zia et al. 2026) was reconstructed from published methods, author-supplied information and author-approved implementation choices. RNA was uppercased, converted from U to T, and represented by separate A/C/G/T position sets (k=1). Vietoris–Rips complexes had maximum simplex dimension one. Graded Betti numbers of S/I over QQ were sampled at ε=0,…,9, excluding β(0,0). Event states were assigned by EPS_AT_RIGHT and carried forward as complete maps; entries absent from a new state became zero. For 157 of 172 unique RNA sequences, features came directly from Macaulay2 caches. Fifteen long sequences used an exact Hochster-formula computation specialized to the ordered one-dimensional position graph after a 22-vertex Macaulay2 state exceeded 900 seconds. The implementation matched all 2,678 cached Macaulay2 state tables checked, with zero discrepancies.

Frozen `esm2_t36_3B_UR50D` supplied layer-36 mean residue representations (2,560 dimensions), excluding special tokens and padding. The fixed computation used FP16 and the first 1,022 protein residues; RNA was retained in full. Each training fold defined its RNA column dictionary, all-zero-column mask and StandardScaler. The GBDT used 10,000 trees, learning rate 0.01, depth 7, minimum split size 3, subsample 0.7, square-root feature sampling, squared-error loss, Friedman-MSE criterion and no early stopping. Fifteen fits used the existing five family folds and seeds 20260916, 20260917 and 20260918. The 406-unit training manifest retained seven historical boundary labels; formal evaluation excluded these seven units and six affected pairs. Training used scikit-learn 1.3.2. Test features did not expand the training dictionary: 98 dictionary-external keys affected 12 formal units. Protein truncation affected seven formal units, including five protein mutations beyond residue 1,022; two formal pairs had identical consumed WT/mutant inputs. These diagnostics were retained with the primary results.

For all three methods, native ΔG (kcal/mol) was converted as pKd = −ΔG/1.3635606572452885 at a reference temperature of 298 K. Pair differences use mutant minus WT; positive ΔpKd and negative ΔΔG indicate stronger binding. Cache identities include the consumed tokens and model configuration, so original sequences that become identical after truncation share an input identity. Two such WT–mutant pairs were retained; their identical consumed inputs yield exactly zero predicted change.

### PRdeltaGPred

A local port retained the released regression parameters (Hong et al. 2023) and used a recorded mkdssp replacement for the bundled DSSP binary. WT and mutant inputs followed the fixed target-residue rebuilding and splicing procedure. Of 348 frozen candidates, 272 passed construction and geometry checks and 256 had successful predictions at both endpoints. Separating eleven 1JBR position controls and ten Hfq source aliases left 235 pairs, 286 absolute units and 153 B-shape pairs. CoPRA and additive references use identical retained members and rebuilt weights (Table S6).

### PRA-Pred

PRA-Pred (Harini et al. 2024) was applied using the released ss+str and ds+enz equations. The ss+str branch uses internal interaction features; ds+enz additionally uses 3DNA geometry (Lu and Olson 2003) and FoldX interaction terms (Delgado et al. 2019). Local adapters handle process isolation, chain verification, file staging and local feature extraction. The original ss+str NO1-key behavior is preserved. Every successful endpoint was checked for its exact input SHA and chain sets, successful exit, complete logs, process cleanup, finite energy, unrounded branch-equation agreement and agreement with the two-decimal result file.

Branch classification used the archived author Dataset table at `outputs/model_round30a_prapred_cohort_preparation/restricted/prapred_dataset.html`. The table lists PDB, RNA strand type, protein functional class, experimental free energy and source, and identifies the collection as data used for training and testing. It provides no row-level training/test indicator. All 17 PDBs in the 138-pair eligible pool occur in this table because branch assignment used these source records. This establishes structure-list correspondence. Assignment to an author's training or test partition and record-level correspondence to our measured constructs were not adjudicated. These results characterize the released equations on structures with documented author-dataset correspondence.

The ss+str and ds+enz applications produced 117 complete pairs; 23 new endpoints failed during 3DNA/FoldX processing and remain missing. The 11 1JBR position controls are separated, leaving 106 main pairs. Published coefficients are retained, and unrounded outputs supply the quantitative evaluation.

### M2-PRNet

We applied M2-PRNet with the released full multimodal architecture and all five author checkpoints (GitHub revision 8353164d2d4f9244c6fce6ea6bab4e35710c08cd; Hugging Face revision 1b895b2330ed056bc973d55ad64a3a01af418cf0). A released sample passed strict checkpoint loading and repeated full-input inference. All 1411 released labels matched binding free energy in kcal/mol. For the external application, each endpoint prediction was the arithmetic mean of the five checkpoint outputs. We converted G to pKd as −G/(RT ln 10), using 1.3635606572452885 kcal/mol at 298 K, and computed ΔpKd from mutant minus WT endpoint pKd. This five-checkpoint external ensemble was fixed before cohort scoring.

New structures used the fixed author graph builder, ESM2-t33 protein and RiNALMo-giga RNA embeddings, computed DSSP and three PyMOL views. The strict image adapter indexed complete PDB/model keys and rejected missing or empty images. Each WT–mutant group shared the WT-derived camera. All embeddings were real, with chain lengths checked before lookup. Image and residue branches ran on CUDA; atom graphs and fusion ran on CPU. Agreement with the CPU official-sample output was within 1.91 × 10⁻⁶ kcal/mol. An exact 8 Å spatial query replaced the quadratic pocket loop after graph and feature equality checks on all four successful pilot endpoints.

Released data and the current author builder differ in angle, secondary-structure and embedding metadata conventions. New inputs retained the current author angle calculation, used computed DSSP and followed the released categorical molecule indicator, which is [1, 0] for both molecule types in all 1411 released entries. The new-structure results therefore evaluate the documented reconstruction with released checkpoints. The archived official-sample forward validates the original input path separately. The revised application uses complete residue index alignment at unchanged checkpoint weights (S7). The checkpoint training-time ordering is not established by the available metadata.

The 348-pair candidate table supplied the scope ceiling. Input-based rules froze 231 pairs and 270 unique endpoints before new cohort scores. Unsupported or failed structures retained their status. 1JBR position controls were kept separately from experimental mutation effects. For method comparisons, CoPRA, its additive comparator and the zero-change reference were recomputed on the same successful experimental pairs. Absolute endpoints were deduplicated by measurement-unit ID, including each shared WT once. CoPRA and additive results average metrics across the three fixed seeds; M2 uses one five-checkpoint ensemble.

We averaged ordinary and centered absolute errors equally over substituted positions within landscapes, equally over landscapes within families, and equally over families. The local subset required at least five distinct positions after the common-member intersection. Direction accuracy excludes observed or predicted zero changes, with coverage and a strict score counting predicted zeros as misses reported separately. Ranking is the Spearman correlation of position-mean effects within eligible landscapes; response amplitude is the position-weighted predicted-to-observed SD ratio, averaged over defined landscapes. Training-set PDB overlap is an annotation of structure exposure. Measurement overlap and construct correspondence retain their source-evidence status.

The local inference runtime used DGL 1.1.2 on CPU and PyTorch 2.9.1 with CUDA 12.8 for the hybrid path. Four validated pilot endpoints were reused from CPU PyTorch 2.13 inference; the remaining 255 successful endpoints used the hybrid path. Package versions and process-specific numerical checks accompany the results.

### mCSM-NA

mCSM-NA (Pires and Ascher 2017) used PDB-ID inputs, RNA as the nucleic-acid type, and author-chain/residue mutation identities. The service returned affinity changes for 251 unique structure–mutation inputs corresponding to 267 database pairs. Input exclusions comprised 69 multi-model constructs, eleven mutations with missing side-chain atoms and one missing target RNA chain. Separating eleven 1JBR position controls and ten Hfq aliases leaves 246 source-deduplicated pairs, including 157 B-shape pairs. The same retained members and weights are used for CoPRA and additive comparisons (Table S7).

Native affinity changes are positive for increased binding and were divided by RT ln 10 at 298 K to obtain ΔpKd. The affinity output supplied the mutation-effect prediction. CoPRA and additive metrics used identical members, hierarchical error weights and seed-wise averaging. Position-mean ranks and position-weighted SD ratios were averaged equally across landscapes. Direction analysis used experimental-nonzero pairs and reported strengthening sensitivity, weakening specificity and balanced accuracy. The service retrieved its PDB structure; CoPRA used the project's prepared structural input (Supplementary Section S4).

### PANTHER

We applied PANTHER (Aletayeb et al. 2026) to 87 mutation pairs across 23 PDB entries, 25 landscapes and 20 families. Input selection retained single-copy structures or mutations mapped to the RNA-contacting copy. All 110 endpoints were scored, including 14 cached responses. The sign-aligned response was −(score_mutant − score_WT), so positive values indicate the strengthening direction. Evaluation retained the native interaction-score units and used zero-response frequency, direction and rank association.

Strict direction agreement required matching nonzero experimental and predicted signs. Identical subsets were used for all methods: 87 total pairs, 85 experimental-nonzero pairs and 40 pairs with nonzero PANTHER and experimental responses. Three-class agreement also counted zero–zero matches. Within landscapes, effects were averaged at each mapped position before Spearman calculation. Five landscapes had at least five positions; four had defined ranks for all methods and seeds. Correlations were averaged within family, across families and then across seeds. Constant-response landscapes contributed to defined-rank coverage. Supplementary S4 gives counts and weighting sensitivity. In separate 1JBR position controls, two substitutions produced zero changes on non-contacting copy A and nonzero changes on RNA-contacting copy B; service metadata identified B–D contact in both settings. These controls remain outside the 87-pair comparison.
